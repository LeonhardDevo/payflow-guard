# Security Policy

This document describes how to report vulnerabilities, how the published security contact files are produced, and the procedure the operator follows when a security incident or personal data breach occurs. Contact details are configuration, not hard-coded: they come from `config/legal.php` and `config/product.php` (environment variables), so every deployment publishes its own operator.

## 1. Reporting a vulnerability

- Send reports to the security contact published in `/.well-known/security.txt` (the operator's company e-mail from `config/legal.php`). Do not open public issues for vulnerabilities.
- Include: affected URL or component, steps to reproduce, impact, and any proof-of-concept. Do not include real personal data of other users.
- We acknowledge receipt within 3 business days, provide an initial assessment within 10 business days, and keep the reporter informed until the issue is resolved.
- Target remediation times after triage: critical 7 days, high 30 days, medium 90 days, low at the next planned release.
- Coordinated disclosure: please allow us up to 90 days (or until a fix is released, if earlier) before public disclosure. We credit reporters who wish to be credited.

### Safe harbour

Good-faith research that follows this policy is authorised. Researchers must: test only against their own accounts and workspaces; stop and report as soon as they access data that is not theirs; not degrade the service (no denial-of-service, no automated high-volume scanning, no spam through messaging features); not use social engineering or physical attacks; keep findings confidential until fixed.

### Out of scope

Missing security headers without demonstrated impact, clickjacking on pages without state-changing actions, self-XSS, rate limits on non-sensitive endpoints, reports produced only by automated scanners, vulnerabilities in third-party services (report them to the vendor), and issues that require a compromised device.

## 2. Published files

| Path | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `/.well-known/security.txt` | RFC 9116 file: `Contact` (mailto of the operator's security e-mail), `Expires` (rolling, at most one year ahead), `Preferred-Languages: en, ru`, `Canonical`, `Policy` (link to `/legal/aup` and this policy's public summary) | generated by the web app from `config/legal.php` and `APP_URL` |
| `/robots.txt` | allows marketing and `/legal/*` pages, disallows application, settings, internal and API paths | web app |
| `/legal/*` | legal documents in English and Russian | `apps/web/resources/legal/{en,ru}` |

The `Expires` field must always be in the future; it is computed at request time so it never goes stale.

## 3. Security baseline (summary)

- TLS everywhere; HSTS in production; strict Content Security Policy (`default-src 'self'`), no third-party scripts, fonts or images.
- Passwords hashed (bcrypt/argon2 via Laravel), optional TOTP two-factor authentication and passkeys; re-authentication for destructive privacy actions.
- Sensitive columns (two-factor secrets, recovery codes, provider API keys) encrypted with the application key.
- Workspace isolation enforced by global scopes and policies; role-based permissions.
- Service-to-service HTTP signed with per-pair HMAC keys, timestamped, replay-protected; internal endpoints are not exposed publicly.
- Inbound webhooks verified (Stripe signatures; YooKassa IP allow-list plus API re-fetch; Telegram secret token) and processed idempotently.
- Structured logs without secrets or prompt contents containing credentials; audit log for security-relevant actions.
- AI: external providers off by default per workspace; PII redaction before any external call; no training on customer data.
- Automated retention purge (see `docs/PRIVACY_ENGINEERING.md`).

## 4. Incident and breach response runbook

### 4.1 Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Incident lead | coordinates the response, keeps the timeline, decides on escalation |
| Engineering responder | containment, investigation, remediation, evidence preservation |
| Privacy contact / DPO (`legal.company.dpo`) | assesses personal data impact, prepares regulatory and customer notifications |
| Communications | customer and public communication, status updates |

In a small team one person may hold several roles, but the privacy assessment must always be done.

### 4.2 Timeline at a glance

| Clock | Action | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | Incident detected or reported; open an incident record (time, reporter, first facts) | — |
| T0 + 1 h | Triage: severity, affected systems, whether personal data may be involved | — |
| T0 + 12 h | When acting as processor for Russian personal data: notify affected customers (operators) | DPA section 9.1 |
| T0 + 24 h | Russian personal data: notify Roskomnadzor via the portal pd.rkn.gov.ru (incident, presumed causes, harm, measures taken, contact person) | 152-FZ art. 21(3.1) |
| T0 + 48 h | Acting as processor (GDPR): notify affected customers (controllers) | DPA section 9.1 |
| T0 + 72 h | GDPR, acting as controller: notify the lead supervisory authority unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals; Russian personal data: send Roskomnadzor the results of the internal investigation and the persons responsible, if identified | GDPR art. 33; 152-FZ art. 21(3.1) |
| Without undue delay | Notify affected individuals when the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms | GDPR art. 34 |
| T0 + 30 days | Post-incident review completed and corrective actions tracked | — |

"T0" is the moment the operator becomes aware of the breach with reasonable certainty. If not all information is available within a deadline, notify on time with what is known and supplement in phases.

### 4.3 Steps

1. **Detect and record.** Create the incident record. Preserve logs (application, audit, worker and RAG API, infrastructure) and avoid destroying evidence.
2. **Contain.** Revoke or rotate compromised credentials (`APP_KEY` rotation plan, `INTERNAL_KEY_*`, provider API keys, Stripe/YooKassa keys, Telegram bot token), disable affected integrations, block malicious IPs or accounts, put the affected feature in maintenance mode if needed.
3. **Assess.** Determine what data, which workspaces and how many individuals are affected; whether data was encrypted; whether it was confidentiality, integrity or availability; likely consequences. Use the data map in `docs/PRIVACY_ENGINEERING.md`.
4. **Decide on notifications** using the table above. Record the decision and reasoning even when no notification is required (GDPR art. 33(5)).
5. **Notify.** Templates must include: description of the incident, categories and approximate number of data subjects and records, likely consequences, measures taken and proposed, recommendations to individuals (for example change password, watch for phishing), contact point.
6. **Eradicate and recover.** Fix the root cause, restore from backups if necessary, verify integrity, monitor for recurrence.
7. **Review.** Blameless post-incident review, timeline, root cause, corrective actions with owners and dates; update this runbook and the DPIA screening if the risk picture changed.

### 4.4 Breach register

Every personal data breach is recorded, including those not notified: date and time detected, description, data and subjects affected, consequences, measures, notification decisions with timestamps, and references to correspondence. Keep the register for at least 5 years.

## 5. Dependency and secret hygiene

- Dependencies are pinned through lock files (`composer.lock`, `package-lock.json`) and updated regularly; security advisories are reviewed on every update.
- Secrets live only in environment variables or encrypted settings; `.env` files are never committed.
- Keys are rotated on staff changes and after any suspected exposure.
